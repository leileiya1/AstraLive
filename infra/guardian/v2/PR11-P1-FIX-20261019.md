## Exact safety decision

Agent4's P1 is correct. Parent `cgroup.procs` omits subgroup members, while
`cgroup.kill` affects the subtree. No kernel or userspace fact in the previous
helper excluded concurrent subgroup creation, member migration, same-UID writers,
inherited writable handles or a privileged lifecycle writer. A second scan,
chmod, local lock or `cgroup.max.descendants=0` snapshot cannot prove that
exclusion. Kernel semantics: [official cgroup-v2 documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#core-interface-files).

`OwnedCgroup` retains real cgroup2 recognition, native inode/device/UID/permissions,
regular non-symlink interface checks, unknown member rejection and registered
PID+birth/UID/direct-parent verification. It checks for subdirectories/symlinks
and repeats scope validation after member reads. These detect bad snapshots;
they do **not** authorize a mutation.

There is no implemented trustworthy exclusive mutation controller. Therefore:

* Constructor validates the proposed parent, then refuses **before mkdir**;
  no old group is accepted and no unmanageable new group is left behind.
* `attach` refuses before any `cgroup.procs` write; it cannot adopt a PID.
* `fence` refuses before any `cgroup.kill` write. No kill write or positive
  fence receipt exists in this implementation, including for an empty leaf.
* `close` refuses before rmdir, even after an empty census. Unknown ownership
  is not converted into permission to remove resources.
* Attempted/failed handles retire locally; process exit, repeated Fence and
  lifecycle races cannot reopen them. The local lock serializes local methods
  only; it is never represented as a kernel topology lock.

There is no new Authority protocol, success flag or supplied callback that turns
these checks into exclusive control. Since no destructive mutation is reachable,
unknown topology inserted after the last snapshot cannot enter a kill scope.
**This is an intentionally unavailable cgroup mutation capability.** Agent4 must
review that limitation rather than treat an inert leaf model as Linux fencing.

A future independently reviewed host controller must exclude all untrusted
topology/member writers across validation and mutation, retain pinned ownership
through lifecycle changes, and then require actual kernel `populated=0` after
an authorized operation. No such positive proof is produced here. Even that
future proof would not revoke RTMP/WebRTC connections, credentials or sockets.
The production PhysicalSource remains an unconditional denial.
